Utilities and Public Works in Portsmith

Public Works maintains water, sewer, and road infrastructure across all
six neighbourhoods, and most resident interactions with the department
happen one of two ways: reporting a problem, or getting advance notice of
planned work that will affect their street. Both go through the same
non-emergency service request line and the online portal, which logs
every request with a tracking number so residents can check on status
without having to call back and re-explain the issue.

Water main breaks and other emergency repairs get prioritized ahead of
routine maintenance, and crews aim to restore water within a few hours for
most breaks, though older sections of pipe in Old Town and the Harbour
District occasionally require longer repairs due to the age of the
infrastructure underneath those streets. When a repair affects water
pressure or requires temporarily shutting off service to a section of a
neighbourhood, Public Works issues a boil water advisory as a precaution
until follow-up testing confirms the water meets safety standards — this
is standard procedure any time pressure drops significantly, not a sign
that contamination has actually been detected.

Road resurfacing follows an annual cycle, with priority given to streets
showing the most drainage or surface damage from the previous year,
particularly after a wet winter or spring flooding. The city tries to
coordinate resurfacing with other planned work on the same street — a bike
lane extension, a water main replacement — so that a single street isn't
dug up and repaved twice within the same year for unrelated projects.
Residents on a street scheduled for work receive a mailed notice roughly
two weeks before crews arrive, with an estimated timeline that Public
Works updates on the portal if conditions cause delays.

Trash and recycling collection runs on a weekly schedule that varies by
neighbourhood, with recycling picked up every other week on the same day
as trash. Bulk item pickup — furniture, appliances, large yard waste —
requires scheduling through the portal at least three business days in
advance rather than simply leaving items at the curb, since unscheduled
bulk items are treated as illegal dumping and can result in a fine to the
property. Yard waste is collected separately from spring through fall and
composted at the city's facility near the Industrial Port.

Streetlight outages, potholes, and storm drain blockages can all be
reported through the same portal used for water issues, and each report
type routes to the appropriate crew automatically rather than requiring
the resident to know which department handles what. Potholes reported
through the portal are typically patched within one to two weeks outside
of winter, when patching material doesn't cure properly in freezing
temperatures and repairs are limited to temporary cold-patch fixes until
weather allows a permanent repair.

Flood mitigation has become a growing focus for Public Works, particularly
in low-lying sections of Riverside, after several seasons of spring
flooding damaged roads and put pressure on the aging storm drain system.
The city's long-term plan includes upgraded storm drains, a new retention
basin, and selectively raised roadbeds on the streets that flood most
often, funded in part through state infrastructure grants the city
applies for annually.
