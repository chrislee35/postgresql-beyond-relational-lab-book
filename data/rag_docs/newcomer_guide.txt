A Newcomer's Guide to Portsmith

Welcome to Portsmith. This guide covers the basics every new resident
ends up needing to look up in the first few weeks — utilities, trash day,
parking, and a rough feel for the six neighbourhoods that make up the
city.

The Harbour District is the oldest part of the city, built up around the
working harbour, and today mixes seafood restaurants, small hotels, and
the waterfront green space with the last of the active dockyards. It's
the most tourist-facing neighbourhood and, correspondingly, has the
tightest street parking, especially in summer.

Old Town sits just inland from the harbour and is Portsmith's historic
commercial core — narrow streets, nineteenth-century storefronts protected
under a historic preservation overlay, and the Saturday farmers market.
Many of the city's oldest independent shops and restaurants are here.

Riverside runs along the river on the city's western edge and is
primarily residential, with the new dog park, several parks and green
spaces, and a mix of older homes and newer apartment buildings. It's also
the neighbourhood most affected by seasonal flooding, so residents there
should pay attention to storm advisories more closely than elsewhere in
the city.

Northgate is a mostly residential neighbourhood on the north side of the
city, home to a growing share of the city's affordable housing
developments and one of the two community garden sites. It has good bus
access to downtown but is the neighbourhood furthest from the harbour and
the university.

The University Quarter surrounds Portsmith University and has the city's
highest concentration of rental housing, along with the bulk of its
nightlife — restaurants, bars, and music venues catering to students and
the surrounding community alike. Noise ordinance enforcement is more
actively watched here than in other neighbourhoods given the venue
density.

The Industrial Port is the smallest neighbourhood by population and the
largest by land dedicated to light industry, warehousing, and the working
port facilities the city was originally built around. It has been
gradually rezoning small parcels for mixed-use development as older
industrial buildings go vacant.

New residents need to set up water service directly with the city — it
isn't automatic when you move in — and should expect trash and recycling
day to depend on which neighbourhood, and sometimes which specific block,
they live on rather than a single citywide schedule. The city's online
portal is the fastest way to find your specific collection day, request a
recycling bin, or set up a new water account, and the same portal handles
business licensing, parks reservations, and non-emergency service
requests, so it's worth bookmarking early.

Street parking rules vary significantly between neighbourhoods: the
Harbour District and Old Town both have metered or permit parking in
their commercial cores, while Riverside, Northgate, and the University
Quarter are largely unmetered but increasingly require a residential
permit for overnight parking in the densest blocks. Checking current
signage on your specific block is more reliable than assuming a rule
based on the neighbourhood in general, since permit zones have been
expanding gradually rather than all at once.
