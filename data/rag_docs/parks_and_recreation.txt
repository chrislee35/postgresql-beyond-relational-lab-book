Parks and Recreation in Portsmith

Portsmith's parks system spans all six neighbourhoods, from the harbourside
green space along the waterfront in the Harbour District to the newer dog
park on Tidewater Lane in Riverside. Every park is free and open to the
public from dawn to dusk, and none require a reservation for casual use —
reservations only come into play for group activities like birthday
parties, sports league practices, or events expecting more than twenty-five
people in one spot.

The Riverside dog park, opened after years of resident requests, is the
only fenced off-leash area in the city and includes separate sections for
small and large dogs, along with water stations and waste bag dispensers
that Parks & Recreation restocks daily. Dogs must be currently vaccinated
and wearing identification tags to use the off-leash area, though the
park has no staff checking tags at the gate — compliance is on the honor
system, backed by posted signage explaining the rules. A second dog park
has been discussed for Northgate but is not yet funded or scheduled.

Facility reservations — picnic shelters, the amphitheater in Old Town, the
sports fields near the University Quarter — go through the same online
portal as business licenses, under the Parks & Recreation section. Popular
shelters book up weeks in advance for summer weekends, so residents
planning a large gathering should reserve as early as the portal allows,
which is currently ninety days out. Reservation fees are modest and go
directly back into shelter and field maintenance rather than the general
fund.

The Old Town farmers market runs every Saturday morning from spring
through early autumn, set up along Market Street and Fisherman's Row.
Vendors need a separate seasonal vendor permit, distinct from a standard
business license, and spots are assigned on a first-come basis at the
start of each season with a modest number reserved for first-time local
vendors to keep the market from being dominated entirely by established
sellers. Market days come with modified parking rules on the surrounding
streets, posted a week in advance.

Youth recreation programs — summer day camps, after-school sports leagues,
swim lessons at the University Quarter pool — open registration in
rolling windows throughout the year rather than all at once, and
financial assistance is available for qualifying families through an
application separate from the general registration form. Space in the
most popular programs, especially summer camps, fills quickly, and the
department maintains a waitlist rather than turning families away outright
once a session is full.

Community garden plots are available in Northgate and the Industrial
Port, on formerly vacant lots the city converted specifically for this
purpose. Plots are assigned by annual lottery each spring, and gardeners
who maintain their plot in good standing get priority for renewal the
following year, though the city reserves a portion of plots for new
applicants every cycle to keep the waitlist moving. There is no cost for a
plot beyond a small refundable deposit tied to basic upkeep.
